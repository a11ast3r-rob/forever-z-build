# 144k Mechanical Baseline — 2013 370Z NISMO

Status: **arrival / first-month evidence plan**

The car has 144,007 advertised miles, one-owner CARFAX history, and 46 reported service records. The right approach is neither “replace everything” nor “assume everything was done.” We start with what is documented, verify the car, then reset only the clocks that are actually unknown or overdue.

## What the history already proves

CARFAX documents:
- Regular oil/filter maintenance across the car's life, with a recent oil/filter service reported on 2026-05-22.
- Front and rear brake work plus four tires/alignment at 55,665 mi in 2017.
- Clutch slave cylinder replaced at 56,535 mi in 2018.
- Major maintenance at 73,527 mi in 2019 including brake-fluid service, differential/rear-differential fluid service, power-steering fluid service, battery, filters, tires and alignment.
- Tires + four-wheel alignment again at 89,649 mi in 2021.
- Repeated oil/filter services through at least 106,801 mi in 2022.
- Minor front/undercarriage damage in 2018 and a second minor-damage report in 2023.

CARFAX does **not** prove current condition, nor does it show every repair ever performed.

## Baseline philosophy

1. Inspect before replacing.
2. Treat undocumented age-sensitive fluids and service items as due until receipts prove otherwise.
3. Do not replace healthy NISMO-specific hardware just because the odometer reads 144k.
4. If a planned upgrade will replace a worn part soon anyway, do not buy the same part twice unless safety demands it now.
5. Record all measurements, photos, fluids, part numbers and mileage so 144k becomes our known starting point.

## Engine — VQ37VHR

### First checks
- Cold start: timing/VVEL noise, smoke, idle stability and warning lights.
- Full OBD scan before clearing anything.
- Inspect valve-cover, front-cover, oil-pan and rear-main-area leakage.
- Check oil level/condition and start an oil-consumption log.
- Inspect serpentine belt, tensioner, idlers, PCV hoses/valves and vacuum hoses.
- Check engine mounts for collapse/cracks and excessive movement.
- Cooling-system pressure test.
- Confirm both radiator fans operate correctly.
- Inspect radiator fins/end tanks, cap, expansion tank and all coolant hoses.
- Compression test as a one-time high-mileage baseline if practical; add leak-down only if compression, consumption, smoke or running quality gives us a reason.
- Mechanical oil-pressure test is optional baseline evidence and becomes mandatory if warning lights, VTC codes, abnormal noise or pressure concerns appear.

### 2013 gallery-gasket context
2013 is the later production era associated with Nissan's updated oil-gallery gasket design, so this is **not** the same proactive-teardown concern as the early Z34 cars. Do not open a healthy engine without evidence.

### Likely baseline service unless receipts prove it
- Spark plugs: Nissan's 2013 maintenance guide calls for NISMO 370Z spark-plug replacement at 120,000 miles. CARFAX does not show that service in the records we have.
- Engine air filters: inspect/replace as needed.
- Cabin filter: inspect/replace.
- PCV valves: inexpensive age/mileage item; inspect and likely renew.
- Drive belt: replace if age is unknown and condition is not excellent.

## Cooling / heat exchangers

### What this 2013 NISMO already has
The 2013 VQ37VHR has a factory **engine oil cooler assembly** (Nissan 21305-JK25A application) that exchanges heat with engine coolant. The car also has a factory **power-steering oil cooler**.

So we are **not starting with “no oil cooler.”**

### Arrival inspection
- Confirm the factory engine oil cooler/heat exchanger is present and dry.
- Inspect its coolant hoses, clamps and oil-cooler gasket area.
- Inspect the factory power-steering cooler and lines for seepage/damage.
- Inspect radiator condition and airflow path, especially because of the prior front/undercarriage damage.
- Verify coolant condition/age and pressure-test the system.
- Inspect heater-core/bleeder connections and aging plastic fittings.

### External oil-cooler upgrade
For a street/highway NISMO, the factory coolant-to-oil unit stays unless there is a specific reason to remove it.

A **thermostatic external air-to-oil cooler** remains a likely longevity/fun upgrade if real summer/spirited driving shows oil temperature needs more control.
- Quality core.
- Thermostatic sandwich plate.
- Proper AN hose/fittings.
- Protected routing.
- Serviceable mounting.
- Leak inspection after heat cycles.

Do not buy the external kit before we confirm what is already on the car and log real oil temperature.

### “Heat exchanger” note
If by heat exchanger we mean the engine-coolant heat exchanger, that is the radiator plus the factory coolant-to-oil cooler. There is **no charge-air/intercooler heat exchanger needed on the naturally aspirated VQ37VHR**. A separate front-mounted charge-cooler heat exchanger only becomes relevant if the car is supercharged/turbocharged later.

## 6-speed manual transmission + clutch

### Known history
The CSC/slave was replaced at 56,535 mi. That is good history, but it is now roughly 87k miles behind the current odometer.

### Arrival checks
- Clutch engagement height and consistency.
- Hot vs cold pedal return.
- Slip test under load.
- Release/throwout noise.
- Master-cylinder seepage.
- Fluid color.
- Shifter play.
- Gear engagement in every gear.
- 1–2 and 5–6 synchro behavior cold and hot.
- Transmission case/tailshaft leaks.
- Mount condition.

### Fluid
Nissan calls for regular inspection of manual-transmission oil; with no documented recent change in the history we have, establish our own fluid baseline unless an invoice proves it was done.

If the transmission ever comes out for a clutch, that is when we deliberately choose:
- clutch/flywheel,
- CSC strategy or external CSC conversion,
- rear main seal inspection,
- pilot/release hardware,
- transmission mount if worn.

Do not pull a healthy transmission just because of mileage.

## Differential / rear driveline

Factory hardware includes the NISMO's VLSD.

### Known history
Differential/rear-differential fluid was serviced at 73,527 mi. That is about 70k miles behind the current odometer, so we should plan another service unless later receipts prove one.

### Inspect
- Differential case/cover/seals for leaks.
- Large rear differential bushing for dark fluid streaks, tearing or excessive movement.
- Front diff mounts.
- Differential mount stud/cover.
- Axle/CV boots and play.
- Wheel bearings/hubs.
- Propeller shaft and couplers.
- Clunks on throttle on/off.
- Vibration under load.
- VLSD behavior — do not assume the viscous unit is still as effective as new solely because it is present.

### Direction
- Fresh correct diff fluid as baseline.
- If the rear diff bushing is failed, use a street-oriented replacement rather than a solid race setup.
- Diff brace only if actual movement/wheel hop/power goals justify it.
- Do not replace the VLSD unless testing/behavior gives us a reason or we later choose a deliberate LSD upgrade.

## Suspension / steering / chassis

The car starts with real NISMO hardware: tuned shocks/springs, stabilizer bars, strut brace and body dampers.

At 144k, age matters more than the badge.

### Inspect every corner
- Shock leakage and damping control.
- Spring condition/seating.
- Top mounts.
- Front compression/tension-rod bushings.
- Front upper/lower ball joints.
- Tie rods / steering rack play.
- Sway-bar links and bushings.
- Rear control-arm bushings.
- Rear camber/toe-arm bushings.
- Subframe bushings.
- Wheel bearings/hubs.
- NISMO body dampers/brackets.
- Alignment hardware and evidence of bent parts.
- Tire wear pattern.

### Build interaction
Because air suspension is planned:
- Do not automatically replace healthy NISMO shocks/springs with fresh stock pieces shortly before removing them.
- Fix any unsafe/sloppy ball joint, bushing, hub, tie rod or bent component now.
- Save geometry/camber/toe upgrades for the final air-suspension layout where possible.
- Keep the original NISMO components with the car.

## Stop / monitor / build gates

**STOP / FIX NOW**
- leaks that can cause fluid loss,
- bad tire/brake/hub/ball-joint/tie-rod,
- low oil pressure,
- overheating,
- clutch hydraulic failure,
- major driveline vibration,
- damaged structural/suspension pickup point.

**DRIVE / MONITOR**
- minor seepage,
- aging but still tight bushings,
- mild shock wear,
- cosmetic underbody damage,
- oil temperatures that are elevated but controlled.

**READY TO BUILD**
- no safety faults,
- fluids/maintenance baseline known,
- engine/trans/diff behavior documented,
- suspension geometry and bushings understood,
- cooling data logged.

That is the point where bags, wheels, audio and performance work stop being guesses.
