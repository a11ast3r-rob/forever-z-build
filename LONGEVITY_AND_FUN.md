# Forever Z — Longevity + Fun Master Plan

Current car: **2013 Nissan 370Z NISMO · 6MT · 144,007 advertised miles**

The goal is not to “restore a 144k car” by throwing parts at it. The goal is to turn a well-used, one-owner NISMO into a **known-condition forever project**: safe, mechanically boring when we want it to be, fun when we ask, and easy to service.

For the detailed arrival checklist, known CARFAX work, fluid-reset logic and system-by-system tests, see [MECHANICAL_BASELINE.md](MECHANICAL_BASELINE.md).

## Golden rules

1. Safety before speed.
2. Evidence before replacement.
3. Preserve healthy NISMO hardware.
4. Reset undocumented maintenance clocks, not documented recent ones.
5. If a planned upgrade will replace a worn component soon, avoid buying the same part twice unless safety requires immediate repair.
6. Keep street/highway manners.
7. DIY as much as we can properly learn and execute.
8. Photograph, measure and log everything.
9. Serviceability beats flashy permanent fabrication.
10. 144k is our starting datum, not a verdict.

## What history changes

The CARFAX is unusually useful for a car at this mileage:
- one-owner history,
- 46 service-history records,
- clutch slave cylinder replaced at 56,535 mi,
- brake fluid + differential/rear-differential + power-steering fluid service at 73,527 mi,
- tires/alignment at 89,649 mi,
- repeated oil/filter maintenance and a recent oil service reported 2026-05-22.

That means we **verify first** instead of assuming the car was neglected.

## Engine

Priority is health evidence:
- cold-start behavior,
- OBD scan,
- oil leaks/consumption,
- belt/tensioner/PCV,
- mounts,
- cooling pressure test,
- compression baseline if practical,
- oil pressure only if symptoms/diagnostics justify it or we want one-time baseline evidence.

2013 is from the later VQ37 production period associated with updated oil-gallery-gasket construction, so we are not planning a blind gallery-gasket teardown.

The big likely missing service item is **NISMO spark plugs at 120k**. If no receipt proves they were done, they go on the baseline list.

## Cooling / heat management

This car already has:
- a factory coolant-to-oil **engine oil heat exchanger/oil cooler**,
- a factory power-steering cooler,
- the normal engine radiator/fan system.

Arrival plan:
- inspect the engine oil cooler, hoses and gasket area,
- inspect the P/S cooler and lines,
- inspect radiator/fans and front airflow path,
- pressure-test the cooling system,
- verify coolant age/condition,
- inspect heater hoses/connectors and aging plastic fittings,
- log oil and coolant temperatures in real street/summer use.

A **thermostatic external air-to-oil cooler** remains a likely fun/longevity upgrade if the logged oil temperatures justify it. It is not an automatic Day-1 purchase.

A separate charge-air/intercooler heat exchanger is **not applicable while the VQ37 remains naturally aspirated**. That only enters the plan with future forced induction.

## 6MT + clutch

The prior CSC replacement is good news, but it has roughly 87k miles on it now.

Inspect:
- clutch engagement/slip,
- hot/cold pedal return,
- master cylinder and fluid,
- release bearing noise,
- shifter play,
- synchro behavior,
- transmission leaks and mount.

Without later records, establish our own manual-transmission-fluid baseline.

If the transmission ever comes out for a real clutch need, then we make the clutch/flywheel/CSC strategy one deliberate job instead of piecemeal work.

## VLSD / rear end

The NISMO already has the factory VLSD.

Inspect:
- diff seals/cover,
- large rear diff bushing,
- front mounts,
- axle/CV boots and play,
- hubs/bearings,
- propeller shaft/driveline vibration,
- throttle-transition clunks,
- actual VLSD behavior.

Differential fluid was documented at 73,527 mi, so absent later proof we reset it now.

Do not replace the VLSD because the internet says “upgrade.” Replace/upgrade only if behavior, wear or future power goals justify it.

## Suspension / steering

The car starts with genuine NISMO:
- tuned shocks/springs,
- stabilizer bars,
- strut brace,
- performance body dampers,
- sport brakes.

At 144k, inspect rather than worship the badge:
- shocks/top mounts,
- compression/tension bushings,
- ball joints,
- tie rods/rack,
- sway links/bushings,
- rear control-arm bushings,
- subframe bushings,
- hubs/bearings,
- body-damper mounts,
- alignment/bent-part evidence.

Because air suspension is planned, do **not** automatically buy fresh stock shocks/springs right before removing them. Fix unsafe/sloppy joints and bushings now; let the final air/geometry build handle parts it truly replaces.

## Brakes

Measure, don't guess:
- pad thickness,
- rotor condition/runout if needed,
- caliper boots/pistons/hardware,
- flex hoses/hard lines.

Brake fluid was documented in 2019; unless later paperwork proves otherwise, flush it during baseline.

Preserve the factory NISMO brake package.

## First-month likely reset

Unless receipts prove recent service:
- manual-transmission fluid,
- differential fluid,
- brake fluid,
- clutch fluid,
- coolant if age is unknown,
- power-steering fluid by condition/history,
- NISMO spark plugs if no 120k proof,
- air/cabin filters,
- belt and PCV items by condition.

The recent oil service means **verify oil rather than changing it simply to restart a spreadsheet**.

## Modification gate

We start the fun build when:
- no safety faults remain,
- engine/cooling behavior is known,
- 6MT/clutch are behaving correctly,
- rear end/hubs/bushings are understood,
- maintenance clocks are documented,
- real oil/coolant temperature data exists.

Then wheels, bags, audio, cooling upgrades and later power work are being added to a car we understand — not used to hide a car we don't.
