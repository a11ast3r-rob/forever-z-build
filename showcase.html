The wrenching has started

October 4, 2026
FIRST REAL GARAGE SESSION

Purchase mode is over.

Hood up, hatch open, dash apart and trim coming out. This was the point where Forever Z stopped being a car we had just bought and became a project we were actually building.

The audio system made the biggest leap. Pulling the passenger seat opened up the room needed to route and terminate the new wiring without fighting the cabin the whole time. We adapted the factory Bose harness clips, mounted the NVX five-channel amp neatly in the cubby behind the passenger seat, and got all four cabin speakers playing through the new system. The used KICKER loaded sub did not survive the reality check — channel 5 works, but the sub itself stayed dead even during a direct battery-pop test, so it is going back.

Mechanically, the catalyst CEL is currently off because the battery was disconnected during the stereo work. That reset also wiped readiness, so the plan has not changed: install the six Denso plugs, inspect the wells and coils, drive it normally, then rescan before deciding whether the cats or sensors actually need money thrown at them.

FROM THE GARAGE

The build looks better with the mess included

Used-sub reality check

The hatch was stripped for wiring and sound-deadening work when the used KICKER enclosure went in for testing. The amp channel checked out. The sub did not. Back it goes.

Kenwood goes in

The DMX809S sitting in the factory metal structure before the dash went back together. Not glamorous, but this is where the new system actually starts.

Seat out, wiring in

Pulling the passenger seat was absolutely worth it. The extra room made routing power, speaker wire and signal cable far easier; with more workspace, the driver's seat would have come out too.

Factory Bose harness, repurposed

These are the stock Bose clips we adapted during the bypass. Not pretty in the middle of the job, but they let the new system take over without pretending the factory wiring never existed.

NVX tucked into the cubby

The payoff: the five-channel amp mounted behind the passenger seat in the cubby, with the hardware kept out of the hatch and the install looking tidy.

Bajie, shop supervisor
