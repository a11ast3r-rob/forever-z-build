# Audio + Acoustic Treatment Plan

Status: **confirmed direction for the 2013 370Z NISMO with Bose**

## Goal

Build a strong street-cruiser system with a clean front stage, real sub-bass, lower cabin resonance/noise, and a hidden/serviceable hatch layout shared with the air-management system.

The factory Bose system is useful as the starting point. We do **not** need to rip it out on Day 1.

## Phase A — Arrival / verify before buying audio parts

- Confirm the exact head unit currently installed.
- Confirm Bose amp / speaker wiring has not been hacked by a previous install.
- Test every factory speaker and factory subwoofer.
- Listen for door, hatch and trim rattles at useful bass levels.
- Photograph connectors, amp location, grounds and any aftermarket wiring before changing anything.
- Keep the working Bose system intact until the complete replacement architecture is ready.

## Head unit + camera direction — next audio idea

**Leading candidate:** Kenwood Excelon Reference **DMX958XR**.

This is a **direction / shortlist item, not a purchase authorization**. Recheck current availability, price, exact harnesses and camera choice when we are ready to order.

### Must-have head-unit features
- Wireless Apple CarPlay + Android Auto.
- Clean standard double-DIN-style fit for the Z.
- Strong front/rear/sub preamp outputs for the later DSP/amp system.
- Rear-camera input with automatic reverse switching.
- Good EQ / time-alignment capability.
- Steering-wheel audio-control retention with the correct interface.
- Bose-compatible staged integration so the new head unit can be installed before the full amp/speaker replacement.

### Camera plan
- Add a discreet **backup camera** with the head-unit install unless the arrival inspection unexpectedly shows one already fitted.
- Prefer an OEM-looking mount around the license-plate / hatch area rather than a bulky plate-frame camera.
- Automatic camera display in reverse.
- Leave expansion room for a **future front/curb camera**, especially useful once the car is lowered / on air.

### Disc-media decision
- **DVD: dropped.**
- **CD: nice-to-have only, not a requirement.**
- Do not compromise the screen, camera support, preouts, phone integration or future audio architecture just to retain a CD mechanism.
- If a similarly strong receiver with CD capability appears at the right price later, it can be reconsidered; otherwise the mechless direction wins.

### Stage-one integration
The head unit / backup camera can happen before the full audio build:
**new head unit → factory Bose amp → factory Bose speakers** using the correct Bose integration interface/harness.

Later, when the full system is built:
**head unit → DSP / aftermarket amp(s) → usable factory speaker-wire runs → new components + custom subs**.

## Final signal + speaker-wire architecture

Preferred plan:

**Head unit / source → DSP or clean preamp signal → aftermarket amp(s) → factory speaker-side wiring → new speakers**

- Bypass the Bose amplifier for final aftermarket speaker power.
- Reuse the factory speaker-wire runs to the doors/dash where the wiring is intact and suitable.
- Tie into those runs on the **speaker side of the Bose amp**, rather than treating the Bose amp as part of the new system.
- Run new speaker wire only if the factory run is damaged, altered, inaccessible for the chosen layout, or the final power/active-DSP design gives us a real reason.
- Run **new dedicated OFC power cable** from the battery for aftermarket amplification, fused near the battery.
- Use a proper new ground point / ground distribution.
- Use new signal interconnects as required by the chosen head unit/DSP/amps.
- Run **new dedicated speaker wire to the custom subwoofer enclosure**.
- Do not rely on the factory Bose amplifier power wiring for the new amplifier system.
- Do not casually mix the factory Bose speakers with a new high-power aftermarket amplifier; treat the Bose amp/speakers as a matched factory system until both sides of that path are intentionally replaced.

## Speakers

### Front stage
- Quality component set.
- Door mid-bass drivers.
- Dash tweeters.
- Use the existing Nissan wire paths where practical.
- Final passive-vs-active crossover architecture is decided after we inspect the actual wiring and choose the DSP/amp package.

### Rear speakers
- Low priority.
- Keep for light rear fill only if they improve the final tune.
- De-emphasize or omit them if the front stage sounds better without them.

### Subs
- Subs remain non-negotiable.
- Custom enclosure sized to the selected driver(s), not selected by appearance alone.
- New dedicated subwoofer speaker wire.
- Enclosure, amp rack, air tanks, compressors and management are designed as **one hatch system** so we do not sacrifice serviceability or useful cargo space unnecessarily.

## Sound deadening / acoustic treatment

This happens **before** the final speakers, subs and false-floor installation.

### Doors
- Butyl vibration-damping material on useful resonant door-sheet-metal areas.
- Treat the doors while the panels are already off for the component-speaker install.
- Use foam/decoupling material where trim contact points need it to prevent buzzes and rattles.
- Keep drains, fasteners, service openings and window mechanisms accessible.

### Hatch / trunk area
- Treat the hatch/trunk floor and useful broad resonant panels before the custom air/audio floor is installed.
- Address trim-panel contact points that buzz or rattle.
- Rattle-test before final panels go back in.
- Do not bury tank drains, compressor service points, amp controls/fuses, wiring junctions or removable hardware under permanent treatment.

### Installation supplies / tools
- Butyl sound-deadening material.
- Appropriate foam/decoupling material for trim contact areas.
- Deadening roller.
- Heat gun as needed for clean application.
- Sharp knife / cutting tools.
- Nylon trim tools.
- Spare trim clips.
- Cleaning/degreasing supplies before applying material.

The goal is **controlled resonance and fewer rattles**, not adding weight everywhere just to claim 100% coverage.

## Power + fabrication rules

- Main amplifier fuse near the battery.
- OFC copper for the main power run.
- Protect wiring from abrasion, heat, moving parts and air-system plumbing.
- Keep signal and high-current power routing deliberate.
- Provide compressor and amplifier ventilation.
- Keep drains accessible.
- Label major wiring and photograph routing before panels close.
- Use removable panels and proper threaded mounting points where appropriate.
- No mystery splices or twist-and-tape connections.

## Build sequence

1. Listen to and document the Bose system as delivered.
2. Verify head unit, Bose amp, wiring and speaker condition.
3. Finalize speakers, DSP/amp architecture and sub configuration.
4. Strip the doors/hatch once and perform acoustic treatment.
5. Install dedicated power/ground/signal wiring.
6. Bypass the Bose amp and connect aftermarket amp outputs to the usable factory speaker-side runs.
7. Install/tune the front stage.
8. Fabricate/install the shared hidden audio + air-management hatch system.
9. Install subs and final amp/DSP hardware.
10. Tune the system, rattle-test it, then close the car up for good.

## Decision summary

**Reuse factory speaker wire where it helps us. New wire where current/power/layout actually demands it. Bose is the starting point, not the final amplifier architecture. Sound deadening is part of the audio build, not an optional afterthought.**
