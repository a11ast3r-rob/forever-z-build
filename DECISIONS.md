# Build Decisions

A short record of decisions that shape the whole project.

## Street use wins

This is not a show-only stance build. Aired-out height can be dramatic, but normal driving height must support:
- good alignment,
- proper tire contact,
- highway stability,
- usable clearance,
- and reasonable tire wear.

## Battery tools over pneumatic shop tools

The home garage will favor cordless tools over an air-powered tool ecosystem.

The car's air suspension still uses electric compressors; this decision is only about shop tools.

## Hidden air management

Prefer two smaller reservoirs and hidden packaging over a single large exposed tank if measurements allow.

Reason:
- preserve cargo/audio space,
- keep the hatch visually clean,
- and make the air system feel integrated into the car.

## Audio and air are one packaging problem

Do not design the air installation first and then force the subwoofer system into leftover space. Measure and design them together.

## Custom work must be serviceable

No permanent mystery panels or inaccessible drains/fittings. Brackets, panels, tanks, compressors, manifold, amps, and wiring should be removable and maintainable.

## Final fitment waits for the actual car

Wheel/tire/suspension numbers in this repository are targets, not permission to order blindly. Brake clearance, fender clearance, tire shape, alignment range, and suspension travel will be checked on the real Z.

## Bose wiring: reuse the useful factory runs

The actual 2013 NISMO is Bose-equipped. The final aftermarket system will **bypass the Bose amplifier for speaker power**, but we will reuse intact factory speaker-wire runs to the doors/dash where practical.

New wiring is mandatory for:
- amplifier battery power,
- proper amplifier grounds,
- subwoofer speaker runs,
- and any signal/interconnect path required by the chosen DSP/amp layout.

We only run brand-new door/dash speaker wire if the factory wiring is damaged/modified or the final power/active configuration gives us a real reason. Avoid unnecessary door-jamb wiring work.

## Sound deadening is part of the audio install

Acoustic treatment is not a later cosmetic extra. Do it while the doors and hatch are already apart:
- butyl vibration damping on useful resonant door and hatch/trunk areas,
- foam/decoupling at trim contact points where needed,
- rattle testing before final reassembly,
- preserve drainage and service access.

The audio, sound treatment, false floor and hidden air-management system are one coordinated fabrication project.
