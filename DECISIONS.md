# Build Decisions

A short record of decisions that shape the whole project.

## Street use wins

This is not a show-only stance build. Aired-out height can be dramatic, but normal driving height must support:
- good alignment,
- proper tire contact,
- highway stability,
- usable clearance,
- and reasonable tire wear.

## Battery tools over pneumatic shop tools

The home garage will favor cordless tools over an air-powered tool ecosystem.

The car's air suspension still uses electric compressors; this decision is only about shop tools.

## Hidden air management

Prefer two smaller reservoirs and hidden packaging over a single large exposed tank if measurements allow.

Reason:
- preserve cargo/audio space,
- keep the hatch visually clean,
- and make the air system feel integrated into the car.

## Audio and air are one packaging problem

Do not design the air installation first and then force the subwoofer system into leftover space. Measure and design them together.

## Custom work must be serviceable

No permanent mystery panels or inaccessible drains/fittings. Brackets, panels, tanks, compressors, manifold, amps, and wiring should be removable and maintainable.

## Final fitment waits for the actual car

Wheel/tire/suspension numbers in this repository are targets, not permission to order blindly. Brake clearance, fender clearance, tire shape, alignment range, and suspension travel will be checked on the real Z.

## Bose wiring: reuse the useful factory runs

The actual 2013 NISMO is Bose-equipped. The final aftermarket system will **bypass the Bose amplifier for speaker power**, but we will reuse intact factory speaker-wire runs to the doors/dash where practical.

New wiring is mandatory for:
- amplifier battery power,
- proper amplifier grounds,
- subwoofer speaker runs,
- and any signal/interconnect path required by the chosen DSP/amp layout.

We only run brand-new door/dash speaker wire if the factory wiring is damaged/modified or the final power/active configuration gives us a real reason. Avoid unnecessary door-jamb wiring work.

## Sound deadening is part of the audio install

Acoustic treatment is not a later cosmetic extra. Do it while the doors and hatch are already apart:
- butyl vibration damping on useful resonant door and hatch/trunk areas,
- foam/decoupling at trim contact points where needed,
- rattle testing before final reassembly,
- preserve drainage and service access.

The audio, sound treatment, false floor and hidden air-management system are one coordinated fabrication project.

See [AUDIO_PLAN.md](AUDIO_PLAN.md).

## DIY first, take the time to do it properly

This is a project car, not a rush-to-finish parts list.

Default approach:
- DIY the work we can safely and competently learn to do.
- Measure, template, mock up and document before cutting or drilling.
- Buy tools when they create repeatable capability for later work.
- Use professional help where specialized equipment, safety, alignment, machining, fabrication quality or warranty considerations genuinely justify it.
- Favor removable/serviceable fabrication over shortcuts.
- Do one area properly rather than repeatedly tearing the car apart.

Time is part of the build. The goal is to learn the car, build skills and end up with work we are proud to service later.

## Front-stage first

The actual car has now told us where the audio money belongs.

Observed after delivery:
- front speakers sound shot / weak,
- factory Bose sub still sounds decent.

Decision:
- fix the front stage first,
- keep the Bose sub,
- do not replace working bass merely because the rest of the system is being upgraded.

Current front-stage direction:
- KICKER KSS650 / 51KSS6504 components
- KICKER KEY200.4
- useful door damping / decoupling during the install

The Kenwood DMX809S and its radio/install parts are already ordered.

## Single stealth 10 replaces the dual-8 plan

The old custom dual-8 direction is retired.

If the upgraded front stage eventually exposes the Bose bass as the weak link, the preferred future bass system is:

- Wicked C.A.S. 370Z Corner 1×10 sealed enclosure
- KICKER CompRT 10 / 48CWRT104
- dual-4Ω coils wired to a 2Ω nominal load
- dedicated mono amplifier around 400–500 W RMS @ 2Ω

Why:
- preserves hatch space,
- fits the OEM-plus / stealth goal,
- is simpler to service,
- reduces conflict with future air-management packaging,
- and should provide the clean single-sub bass level this car actually needs.

When the aftermarket 10 is installed, retire / disconnect the factory Bose low-bass path rather than intentionally running two different sub systems over the same range.

## Real-car evidence outranks the old plan

Plans written before delivery are provisional.

Now that the car is home:
- real driving behavior,
- actual temperatures,
- actual sound,
- actual fitment,
- and actual wear

override assumptions made from listings, photos or generic 370Z build recipes.

That rule applies to audio, cooling, tires, bushings, suspension and every future modification.
